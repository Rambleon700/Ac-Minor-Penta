Acoustic Connection System

Finding Your Anchors: Root Note Grid across 5 CAGED Boxes

To solo fluidly without getting stuck in isolated box patterns, you must visualize Root Note Anchors (R) across all 6 strings.

Box 1 & 2 Anchors:

Box 1 anchors on Strings 6, 4, and 1. Sliding into Box 2 shifts your anchor to the 4th and 2nd strings.

Box 3, 4 & 5 Anchors:

Box 3 anchors on String 5 & String 3. Box 4 anchors on String 5 & String 2. Box 5 anchors on String 6 & 1.

The Acoustic Diagonal Slide Connection

Acoustic guitars benefit tremendously from sliding transitions because picking every single note can sound harsh. Diagonal slides create smooth, vocal-like phrasing up and down the fretboard.

The Golden G-String Shift Path (Box 1 ➔ Box 2 ➔ Box 3):

Start on the ♭3 note in Box 1 on String 3 (G string) with finger 1, then slide up 2 frets into the 4th interval of Box 2 with finger 3. From there, hammer-on to the 5th interval.

Double-Stops & 6ths Acoustic Harmony

When playing acoustic guitar solo without a band, double-stops (two notes plucked simultaneously) add harmonic weight and rhythm-lead texture.

Parallel 4ths Double-Stop:

Barre adjacent string pairs (G & B or B & High E) across same frets in Box 1 & Box 4.

Sliding 6ths (G & High E Strings):

Pluck String 3 and String 1 together while skipping String 2 for classic Delta acoustic blues texture.

Acoustic Bending & Micro-Tonal Vibrato

Steel string acoustic guitars typically feature heavy string gauges (.012-.053). Full-step string bends on lower frets can strain your fingers or break strings.

  • The Minor 3rd Curl (Quarter-step Bend): Instead of bending a full step, lightly push the ♭3 note up 1/4 step toward the Major 3rd before resolving to the Root.
  • G-String Half-Step Bends: The G string is easiest to bend. Support the bending finger with 2 fingers behind it!

The Hybrid "BB / Albert King Box" Position

The "King Box" sits right between Box 2 and Box 3 on the top three strings (G, B, High E). It offers effortless access to major and minor pentatonic soloing.

Anchor your index finger on the Root note on String 2 (B String). From this anchor, your ring finger can easily reach the 4th degree, bend up to the 5th, or reach the high Root on String 1.
